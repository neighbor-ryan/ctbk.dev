# GBFS alerts based on coverage and loss risk

Implemented 2026-10-11; updated 2026-10-20 for two-feed coverage and monthly Slack reporting. Harmless CDN POP changes and borderline lag remain observable without repeatedly paging Slack. Alerts prioritize possible loss and conditions that can cause it.

- Removed `feed-pop` paging. A non-US serving POP alone does not establish stale data or missed generations.
- `feed-lag` fires on three distinct health observations first seen at least 60 seconds late within ten minutes. Recovery requires at least three observations and a ten-minute p90 below 45 seconds. Missing samples cannot clear the condition. This primary-feed warning includes combined coverage context.
- `feed-generation-gaps` fires on three minute slots absent from both feeds in ten settled minutes and recovers with at most one. The window spans UTC midnight and excludes the current and previous minute. Slots measure timestamp-minute observations, not an exhaustive census of upstream generations: timestamp phase shifts can leave a slot empty.
- `feed-missed-lus` only considers uncovered slots adjacent to missed primary cron ticks. Backup observations credit recovery. `feed-stale` requires both feeds' upstream timestamps to be stale; a new upload of an old payload does not establish freshness.
- `feed-redundancy` records degradation of one source while combined coverage remains available. It edits the daily report instead of broadcasting. Incomplete listings trigger `feed-coverage-unknown`; stale health snapshots also remain critical. Uncertain coverage preserves existing loss/redundancy conditions rather than manufacturing loss or clearing them.
- Minute refreshes add only today's and yesterday's bounded backup listings to the existing narrow reads. They do not reintroduce historical inventory scans. `health/feed-coverage/YYYY-MM-DD.json` stores settled combined counts and 24 hourly primary/backup/combined summaries, finalizing yesterday after midnight. The last seven daily documents are read by exact key.

Slack reporting follows the monthly-thread pattern used by Disky: one edited monthly OP with Monday-UTC weekly rollups and an editable image block; one edited reply per UTC day containing condition episodes, current/resolved status, and observed coverage. Routine warning flaps and recoveries do not add replies. Critical incidents broadcast once into the monthly thread and their incident message is edited on recovery. Previous days and completed months are explicitly labeled historical. Existing standalone incidents are edited when resolved.

Delivery uses an R2 conditional-write lease and persisted outbox. Successfully acknowledged OP/reply IDs and critical transitions are checkpointed before the next operation, so known retries retain delivery identity and episode counts. Slack and R2 do not share a transaction: a process dying after Slack accepts a new message but before its ID is checkpointed can still duplicate that message. Delivery is at least once, not exactly once; no thread-history scans or edits of human messages are performed.

The `/health/feed` page and `/api/health/card.png` share a bounded SVG renderer. The image reads only the persisted snapshot, uses a minute-normalized edge cache, marks stale snapshots and unmeasured days, and separates primary, backup, and combined coverage. Monthly OP image URLs change with the snapshot timestamp so Slack replaces the image block.

`health/feed-drift.json` retains sampled lag and optional POP observations. Daily `health/feed-history/YYYY-MM-DD.json` summaries retain counts, p50/p90 lag, generation-late observations, and known POP counts. These diagnostics are sampled; overlapping refreshes can lose a point. Immutable raw generation objects, introduced separately in `gbfs-generation-retention.md`, preserve distinct captured updates; legacy minute aliases and normalized latest-observation-per-minute semantics remain compatible.

Validation covers complete/unknown two-feed coverage, missing-cursor pagination, midnight boundaries, frozen payload freshness, warning flaps, overlapping initial runs, persisted retries, critical recovery edits, day/month rollover, weekly totals, and editable Slack image transport. The health page passed both automated Chromium rendering and Chrome CUA verification.

Rollout: the API and monthly reporter are live. At 12:26 UTC, 745/745 settled slots had combined observations, with 124 recovered by backup and zero combined gaps. The reporter persisted one October OP and one October 20 reply, no pending delivery, no active conditions and zero Slack delivery failures. The live PNG was fetched and visually reviewed. Chrome verified the rendered health card and combined-coverage copy at `http://localhost:3478/health`. The pure health model and renderer reside in `www/src/lib/` so the website's standalone screenshot Docker context includes them.
